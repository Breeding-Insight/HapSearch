About Microhaplotypes

Microhaplotypes are short genomic segments (typically 54-109 bp) containing two or more closely spaced variants that are inherited together as a single unit. Unlike single SNPs which are bi-allelic, microhaplotypes are multi-allelic markers that provide significantly higher information content for diversity analysis, QTL discovery, and genomic selection in plant breeding.

Because the SNPs within a microhaplotype are sequenced together in the same short read, their phase is directly observed, making them highly informative, phase-known markers. This multi-allelic nature and strong linkage make microhaplotypes especially powerful for characterizing germplasm, where unique and trait-associated microhaplotypes can be tracked within and across breeding populations to inform designing crosses.

HapSearch Platform

HapSearch centralizes fragmented microhaplotype data generated from the DArTag targeted genotyping platform across multiple breeding programs. The platform enables targeted mining of alleles of interest by crop species, sequence similarity, and project-specific keywords, with integrated filters to narrow results to specific loci. It also provides multiple sequence alignment views to display locus-level variation and help users identify accessions carrying unique microhaplotype alleles, while connecting sequence-level information to breeding projects and samples for marker evaluation and decision support. By improving data discoverability and cross-project visibility, HapSearch supports collaboration and resource exchange across breeding and research communities.
